# What Not To Do: The Bot-Run Rug Pull

*How the scheme works, why it's fraud, how it gets caught, and how to protect yourself.*

---

## 1. The scheme in one paragraph

Someone uses software bots to launch a meme coin that rides a trending topic. They give it a fake "hot" chart using money that moves between wallets they secretly control. Strangers see the fake activity, assume it's real demand, and buy in. The operator then sells everything at once. The price collapses, and the strangers are left holding worthless tokens.

## 2. The five stages (pattern only)

| Stage | What happens | What the public sees |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Hype hook | A coin is named after whatever is trending that day | "Wow, a coin for the thing everyone's talking about" |
| 2. Launch | The creator mints the coin and buys a large share early | A brand-new coin with a few buyers |
| 3. Fake demand | Wallets owned by the same person trade back and forth, buying slightly more than they sell | Rising price, high volume, "lots of holders" |
| 4. Bait | The coin lands on trending lists and gets noticed on social media | FOMO: real people buy in |
| 5. Dump | The insider sells their whole position in minutes | The price falls 90–100% and buyers can't get out |

**The key point:** the operator's wallets only pass money among themselves. **Every dollar of "profit" is money taken from real buyers.** Nothing of value is created.

## 3. Why it's illegal, not just "risky"

- **Wash trading.** Trading with yourself to fake volume is market manipulation.
- **Pump-and-dump.** Pushing a price up with misleading activity, then selling into it, is fraud in the US, UK, EU, Australia and most other countries.
- **Wire fraud and money laundering.** These charges come up when proceeds move through exchanges or banks.
- **Calling it a "token" or "just an experiment" is no defense.** Prosecutors look at intent and at who lost money.

## 4. Real cases

- **Operation Token Mirrors (Oct 2024).** The FBI created its own token (NexFundAI) to catch market makers who sold fake-volume services. Charges were brought against 18 people and companies, and firms such as Gotbit were caught. Gotbit's founder pleaded guilty and went to prison.
- **Squid Game token (2021).** It rode a Netflix trend and rose thousands of percent. Buyers turned out to be blocked from selling, and the developers cashed out about $3M. The price hit roughly zero within minutes.
- **Frosties NFT (2022).** The founders took about $1.1M and disappeared. They were arrested and charged with wire fraud and money-laundering conspiracy.

## 5. How these schemes get caught

1. **Blockchains are permanent and public.** Every wallet, transfer and trade can be traced forever.
2. **Wallet clustering.** Tools like Bubblemaps link wallets funded from the same source. A ring of "different buyers" shows up as one cluster.
3. **Bundle detection.** Trading tools flag coins where one party bought most of the supply at launch.
4. **Exchange KYC.** Cashing out to a regulated exchange ties the wallet to a real name and ID.
5. **Community callouts.** On-chain sleuths publicly expose rugs within hours.

## 6. Red flags for buyers

- The coin is only hours old and named after today's news
- A smooth "staircase" chart with steady volume and few real social posts
- A handful of wallets hold most of the supply
- Holder wallets were all funded from the same source around the same time
- The dev wallet holds a large share, or liquidity isn't locked
- The only marketing is "it's trending, get in now"

**Rule of thumb:** if the only reason to buy is that the price is going up, you're probably the exit liquidity.

## 7. The takeaway

The rug pull looks like a money machine, but it's a transfer of money from trusting people to a scammer, and it leaves a permanent public record pointing back at the scammer. The same skills (trend detection, automation, reading on-chain data) are worth more when used to **detect** these schemes than to run them.
